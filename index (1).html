What a Global Grant is

Funding for projects with lasting, measurable impact

A Global Grant is a Rotary Foundation award, matched from the World Fund, that supports humanitarian projects, vocational training teams, and scholarships in one or more of Rotary's areas of focus. Every grant needs a host sponsor in the country where the work happens and an international sponsor from elsewhere — and every project must be built to outlast the grant itself: locally owned, locally maintained, and measurable against goals the community helped set.

Before you apply

Club qualification checklist

Qualification resets every Rotary year on 1 July. A club can't open or submit a Global Grant until the district has recorded it as qualified for the current year.

Club or district qualified for the current Rotary year

Recorded by the district — confirm this before you start anything else.

Grant Management Seminar completed

Required officers and grant-committee members complete it (or recertify) in Rotary's Learning Center on My Rotary.

Club MOU signed and filed

President and president-elect (or two officers) sign the current-year Club Memorandum of Understanding and any district addendum, filed with the District Rotary Foundation chair before the district deadline.

Dues current

Rotary International and district dues paid in full — unpaid dues block both qualification and payment.

No overdue reports on prior grants

Any outstanding progress or final report on a district or Global Grant can suspend the club from new applications.

Officers listed correctly in Club Central

President, secretary, treasurer, membership chair, and Rotary Foundation chair need to be current so Grant Center routes authorizations to the right people.

Within the sponsor limit

A club or district may be primary sponsor of no more than 10 active Global Grants at a time.

Step by step

The application, start to close

Everything happens inside Grant Center on My Rotary — not by email attachment. Applications are accepted year-round and reviewed as they come in.

01

Confirm qualification and find a partner

Both the host club (where the project happens) and the international club need to be qualified for the year. Identify your primary host and international contacts — both need a My Rotary account under the email the club has on file.

02

Form your committees

Three Rotary members from the host club or district, and three from the international club or district. Both committees stay involved through closeout and are responsible for implementation, spending, and reporting.

03

Start the application in Grant Center

Sign in to My Rotary → Participate → Grant Center → Apply for a Grant → Global Grant. The system assigns a GG number once Basic Information is complete.

04

Build the project

Areas of focus, goals and measures, project overview, budget (minimum $30,000 including the World Fund match), funding sources, and sustainability plan. Save and continue after each section — don't submit a draft until district DDF is confirmed, if you're requesting it.

05

Route for authorization

Primary contacts complete the application, then club presidents authorize, then district leaders (DRFC or DG) authorize, then it's submitted to The Rotary Foundation. Incorrect officer records anywhere in this chain will stall the application.

06

District review, if required

Some districts, including 3790, review the draft application before it goes to TRF. Send the Grant Center PDF and budget calculator to the district Global Grants Subcommittee first.

07

Approval and payment

Once approved, the host sponsor enters the dedicated grant bank account and two signatories in Grant Center. Don't spend anything until the Foundation has allocated the funds.

08

Implement, then report

A progress report is due within 12 months of the first payment; a final report is due within two months of completing activities. Keep every receipt and bank statement for five years.

Know the limits

What Global Grants don't pay for

A short list of the exclusions clubs run into most often — the full terms and conditions cover many more.

  • New construction of, or additions to, any building or structure people live or work in
  • Completing a partially built structure that has never been occupied
  • Equipment or items that would be owned by a Rotary club, district, or member — ownership must sit with the benefiting community or organization
  • Projects that are primarily research or data collection, or whose only expense is travel
  • Undergraduate study or any fully online academic program
  • Multiple unrelated activities bundled under one grant
  • Rotary Youth Exchange, RYLA, Rotary Friendship Exchange, Rotaract, or Interact activities
  • International travel for anyone under 18 without an accompanying parent or guardian
